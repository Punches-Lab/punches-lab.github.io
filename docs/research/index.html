Active & Recent Research

Active Principal Investigator

Decision-Making Factors for Therapeutic Opioid Use after Emergency Care

Funding
NIH/NIDA K08DA049948 — April 2021–March 2026
PI
Brittany Punches, PhD, RN — Mentor: Michael S. Lyons, MD, MPH
Objective
Develop and psychometrically test a decision-making instrument to understand and predict opioid use patterns after ED discharge for acute pain, informing interventions to prevent opioid use disorder.
Methods
Mixed-methods prospective cohort; 3-month follow-up assessments; ED sites across the Midwest
Impact
The Decisions to Use Opioids Measure (DOM) will enable ED clinicians to identify patients at risk for problematic opioid use and deliver targeted, early interventions.
Key Publications
  • Punches BE, et al. (2024). Development and validation of the Decisions to use Opioids Measure. Pain Management Nursing.
  • Punches BE, et al. (2024). Patient motivators to use opioids for acute pain after emergency care. Frontiers in Pain Research.
  • Punches BE, et al. (2022). Predicting at-risk opioid use three months after an ED visit for acute pain. PLOS ONE.
Completed First Author

Screening for Problematic Opioid Use in the Emergency Department

Objective
Compare two validated screening measures (DSM-5 Checklist vs. WHO ASSIST 3.1) for opioid use disorder in ED patients; determine prevalence of OUD and substance use patterns.
Methods
Cross-sectional study; 3 Midwestern ED sites; 1,000+ adult patients; item response theory analysis
Impact
Identifies disparities in OUD screening performance by sex, race, and employment status; informs equitable ED screening protocols.
Publication
Punches BE, Freiermuth CE, et al. (2024). Screening for problematic opioid use in the emergency department: Comparison of two screening measures. JACEP Open, 5(1), e13106.
Active

Longitudinal Assessment of Post-Traumatic Syndromes (AURORA Study)

Funding
NIH/NIMH U01MH110925 — PI: Samuel A. McLean, UNC; OSU Site PI: Brittany Punches
Objective
Prospective, longitudinal cohort examining mechanisms and predictors of adverse post-traumatic outcomes (depression, anxiety, PTSD); role of sociodemographic, psychosocial, and biological factors.
Methods
1,305+ trauma-exposed ED patients; baseline and 3-month assessments; validated instruments (PROMIS, Primary Care PTSD Screen); multiple OSU ED sites
OSU Sub-Studies
  • Brain-based biotypes of psychiatric vulnerability (Stevens et al., American Journal of Psychiatry, 2021)
  • Prediction of post-trauma outcomes using wrist-worn sensors and circadian data (Cakmak et al., IEEE JBHI, 2021)
  • Multi-level socioeconomic modifiers of PTSD-substance use comorbidity (Meyers et al., in preparation)
Completed

Patient Perceptions of Microaggressions and Discrimination in Emergency Care

Objective
Qualitative study exploring patient experiences of discrimination, microaggressions, and unfair treatment in the ED; link to health outcomes and care-seeking behavior.
Methods
Prospective interview study; diverse sample of ED patients; qualitative thematic analysis
Impact
Highlights the need for cultural competency training and systemic anti-racism work in emergency department settings.
Publication
Southerland LT, Punches BE, et al. (2023). Patient perceptions of microaggressions and discrimination toward patients during emergency department care. Academic Emergency Medicine.
Completed Principal Investigator

Thematic Focus

Research Areas

1. Opioid Use Disorder & Pain Management

Understanding therapeutic decision-making, screening, assessment bias, and clinical outcomes related to opioid use in emergency care.

  • How do patients decide whether to use opioids after ED discharge?
  • Are OUD screening tools biased across demographic groups?
  • What factors predict problematic opioid use after emergency care?

Funding: NIH/NIDA K08 (Punches, PI); Emergency Nurses Association Foundation; American Nurses Foundation

2. Trauma Recovery & Post-Traumatic Outcomes

Understanding mechanisms of post-traumatic stress, depression, anxiety, and substance use comorbidity; identifying protective and risk factors.

  • What biological, psychological, and social factors predict adverse outcomes after trauma?
  • How do socioeconomic factors modify trauma-substance use relationships?
  • Can wearable sensor data identify high-risk patients early?

Funding: NIH/NIMH U01 (McLean, PI); AURORA Consortium

3. Substance Use Disorder Prevention & Early Intervention

ED-based screening, assessment, nurse perspectives, linkage to care, and community-based harm reduction.

  • How can EDs better identify and support patients with substance use disorders?
  • What are barriers and facilitators to nurse-driven SUD screening?
  • How can naloxone distribution programs reduce overdose mortality?

Funding: SAMHSA; Ohio Department of Health; Ohio Attorney General; CDC

4. Health Equity in Emergency Care

Cross-cutting commitment to understanding and addressing disparities in pain management, SUD assessment, trauma care, and provider-patient interactions.

  • Are pain assessments and opioid prescribing equitable across race, ethnicity, and gender?
  • Do screening tools have differential performance by demographic group?
  • How do patients experience discrimination and microaggressions in emergency care?
